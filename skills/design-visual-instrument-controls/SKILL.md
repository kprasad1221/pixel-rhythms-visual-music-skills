# Design visual instrument controls

Start with a listening task. Ask someone to enter the piece, play a passage, change one expressive quality, and return to the original look while the music continues. Build the shortest comfortable path through those actions.

1. **Separate performing from editing.** Put transport, the active scene, and a few meaningful controls within reach. Give each macro one audible or visible consequence. In Living Particles, Movement scales lift, Activity scales particle budgets, and Light scales emission. Expose their underlying parameters in an editor grouped by what the performer is trying to change.
2. **Make comparisons reversible.** Show what is playing, which look is being edited, and what has changed since recall. Keep A, Mix, and B explicit. Editing B at mix zero needs visible feedback. Switch a response style or a material without resetting the track. Give Hold and Pause distinct labels if they affect different clocks.
3. **Let the world suggest the instrument.** In Jellyfish Palace, a sea-glass dock contains viewpoint pads, transport, and a volume control. The separate Light and Sound panels give the two edits a clear home. For a different world, choose one material or motif already present in it and use that to shape a small set of legible controls.
4. **Make diagnosis available when needed.** Put raw measurement, processed signal, and visible output next to the mapping being edited. Offer a manual impulse or a solo control if one layer hides another. Return the attention to the piece when the editor closes.
5. **Rehearse with a keyboard and a narrow screen.** Start playback, change a parameter, compare a look, restore the scene, and hide and recover the UI. Confirm that shortcuts leave text entry alone, focus remains visible, and transport remains reachable. Check that the first interaction gives a newcomer something audible or visible to evaluate.

**Try it.** Compare the [Living Particles performance bar](https://github.com/kprasad1221/Living-Particles-3js/blob/c654ae403c5bece4822dd38024228c1fc468acd4/README.md#using-it) with the [Jellyfish Palace instrument dock](https://github.com/kprasad1221/jellyfish-palace-3js/blob/cc8f8e7442d497677bbb440dbcc9136f5112f3d3/README.md#version-03--palace-and-instrument). Sketch the controls your own scene needs during a single eight-bar performance. Move one rarely used control into the editor and repeat the performance.
